Why nobody has shown you this before

You already knew both of these things. Nobody told you they were the same thing.

You know the weekly shop costs more than it used to. You know you're not eating as well as you'd like. You've felt both — separately, and slightly guiltily.

They're the same purchases. The snacks eating the groceries budget are the same snacks scoring Nutri-D. Health apps score one product at a time; budget apps see a line called Supermarkets — €23,13 and stop there. Neither connects them, and the connection is the point. Basket Vitality reads the receipt, so it sees both at once.

What it doesn't do

It doesn't pretend to know things it doesn't.

Basket Vitality is built for Dutch supermarket receipts and sold on Google Play in the Netherlands and Belgium.

Product data comes from Open Food Facts and RIVM's NEVO table, and neither is complete. When a line on your receipt isn't recognised, or a product has no nutrition data behind it, the app says so instead of guessing — and you can fix the match yourself. Climate figures appear only where AGRIBALYSE has them. A swap is a better product in the same category, not a promise that it's on the shelf today. And it doesn't lecture: no streaks, no shame, no daily reminders.

Privacy

Your receipts never leave your phone. Not once.

This is a health and money app. That is the most sensitive pair of things you own, and there is no version of this where we hold them.

No account

There is nothing to sign up for. No email, no password, no profile. You open the app and use it.

Encrypted on your phone

Your receipts, transactions and scores live in an encrypted database on your device, behind your PIN or fingerprint. We have no copy, because there is nowhere to keep one. What does go out: product pictures, an anonymous barcode lookup to Open Food Facts when you ask for one, and Google Play for subscriptions and ratings.

No analytics

Nothing tracks what you scan or what you buy. Crash reports stay on your phone unless you choose to send one.
